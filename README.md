# Journal Club & Research Presentation Guide

Guides and resources for scientific reading, critical thinking, Journal Club discussions, and research presentations.

## Presentation Guides

- **[Journal Paper Presentation Guide](journalclub-journal-paper.md)** — For Nature, Science, Cell, and related journals; focus on the scientific question, conceptual advance, key evidence, mechanism, and significance.
- **[Conference Paper Presentation Guide](journalclub-conference-paper.md)** — For conferences such as NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR, AAAI, RSS, ICRA, and IROS; focus on the problem, technical novelty, method, evidence, limitations, and practical value.

## References & Further Reading

The following resources from **Nature and Nature Portfolio** provide useful guidance and examples for scientific reading, critical thinking, Journal Club discussions, and research presentations. This list will be continuously updated.

### Journal Club

- **[How our student journal club built on sand developed strong foundations](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-025-01606-x)** — *Nature, 2025*  
  A practical example of how a student-led Journal Club can develop from informal paper discussions into a sustainable research community.

- **[Nature Reviews Neuroscience — Journal Clubs](https://www.nature.com/nrn/articles?type=journal-club)**  
  Examples of concise Journal Club articles that focus on **why a paper matters, how it changed scientific understanding, and how it influenced subsequent research**. Particularly relevant for students in neuroscience.

- **[Nature Reviews Psychology — Journal Clubs](https://www.nature.com/nrpsychol/articles?type=journal-club)**  
  Short pieces by graduate students or postdocs explaining a paper that is significant to **their research, themselves, or the broader field**. See the journal's [content types and article descriptions](https://www.nature.com/nrpsychol/content) for its definition of Journal Club articles.

- **[Nature Reviews Physics — Journal Clubs](https://www.nature.com/natrevphys/articles?type=journal-club)**  
  Examples of discussing influential papers through the perspective of **how a study changed the author's thinking or research direction**.

### Critical Reading

- **[Seven steps for critically analysing research papers](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-01209-0)** — *Nature, 2026*  
  Provides a structured approach for moving beyond paper summarization to critically evaluate the **question, evidence, interpretation, assumptions, and limitations**.

- **[My four-step workflow for reading scientific papers (without trying to read them all)](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-02119-x)** — *Nature, 2026*  
  Recommends **discovering, triaging, skimming, and then deeply reading** only the papers that deserve closer attention.

### Scientific Presentation

- **[Why your scientific presentation should not be adapted from a journal article](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-020-03300-6)** — *Nature, 2020*  
  A scientific talk should communicate a **clear story and a few key messages**, rather than reproduce all of the details contained in the paper.

- **[Ways to give an effective seminar about your research project](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-019-01574-z)** — *Nature, 2019*  
  Emphasizes using slides as a **visual roadmap** while keeping the audience focused on the scientific story.

- **[Good presentation skills benefit careers — and science](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-021-01281-8)** — *Nature, 2021*  
  Highlights common problems such as **too much detail, opaque figures, too many slides, and insufficient time for discussion**.

### Key Principle

> **Understand the question → evaluate the evidence → identify the contribution → discuss the limitations → connect the paper to your own research.**
